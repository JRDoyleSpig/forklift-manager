Help & Training
Current operating guide for the full Forklift Manager — fleet, service, PMs, hours, lifecycle, rentals, accounting, budget, grading, and replacement planning.
How the data fits together
Forklift Fleet is the current-state master for each asset. Forklift Transactions is the financial master for money spent. Forklift Service Events records PMs, repair activity, documents, and lifecycle history. Forklift Hour Readings preserves meter history. Forklift Rentals tracks rental status/cost before and after invoices arrive. Forklift Budget and Budget Forecast History support monthly 6022/6023 forecasting. Lease Manager supplies the active-workroom list.

The app intentionally keeps current state, operational history, meter history, financial history, and planning separate. One workflow may update more than one list, but the lists do not replace one another.

Dashboard — what belongs in Needs Attention?

The Dashboard is for fleet condition and operational exceptions, not routine preventive-maintenance scheduling. PM overdue/due-soon items are intentionally managed on the separate PM Schedule page and are not shown in Dashboard Needs Attention.

Location issues, repair/downtime concerns, stale or inconsistent operating data, and other actionable fleet exceptions can still appear on the Dashboard. The fleet counts distinguish operating from inactive/parked assets.

Fleet — All, Active, Inactive, adding and editing forklifts

Forklift Fleet is the current-state master. It stores the permanent Forklift ID, make/model/serial, current workroom, current status, current hours, service vendor, PM fields, attachments/poles, acquisition information, estimated replacement cost, and notes.

Active Forklifts includes Active and Down for Repair. Inactive Forklifts includes Parked, Retired, Scrapped, and Sold. All Forklifts shows the complete fleet.

Forklift IDs are permanent and are never reused. Once a forklift exists, Current Workroom and Status are controlled by the Lifecycle workflows so history is not lost.

Changing Current Hours through Edit Forklift creates an Hour Reading automatically. A lower meter value produces a warning because it may indicate an incorrect prior reading or a meter replacement/reset.

Status definitions

Active: normal operating service. Down for Repair: temporarily unavailable but expected to return to service. Parked: still owned/retained but intentionally not being used or actively repaired; it may return later.

Retired: permanently removed from normal service but may still be physically retained while disposition is decided. Sold: ownership transferred by sale. Scrapped: permanently disposed of as scrap.

Retired → Active must use Reactivate. Sold and Scrapped are final. Disposition Date is entered from the real event date; the app does not invent one.

Transfers, building relocation, status changes, reactivation, and disposition

Forklift Fleet is current state; Lifecycle History is the audit trail. Use the lifecycle buttons in the forklift record instead of directly changing Current Workroom or Status.

Transfer Forklift records From Workroom → To Workroom and updates the Fleet record. The destination workroom's Primary Service Vendor and phone follow the lift. If status also changes, the app creates a separate Status Change event.

Building Relocation is for the same workroom moving to a new building/address. The workroom name remains the same because the property/building information belongs in Lease Manager.

Change Status handles Active, Down for Repair, and Parked. Reactivate is required for Retired → Active. Retire / Dispose records Retirement, Sold, or Scrapped; Sold/Scrapped can store proceeds or scrap value. Disposition proceeds are lifecycle information only and do not reduce repair spend or create a financial transaction.

Lifecycle records are normally never deleted. Use Void / Entered in Error with a reason when a lifecycle record is wrong. Voided records remain visible for audit but are ignored by lifecycle logic.

Historical backfills may be marked Historical Backfill and Date Approximate. Backdated service/NetSuite records use lifecycle history to determine the workroom that applied on the record date, so old costs and service stay at the historical location.

Primary Service Vendor — workroom behavior

Primary Service Vendor is treated as a workroom-level operating relationship. Transfers pick up the destination workroom's vendor. If you intentionally change the primary vendor on one current forklift, the app can synchronize that vendor/phone across the other current forklifts at the same workroom.

PM uploads and repair workflows prefill the current primary vendor, but you may use a different vendor for a single event. A one-off service vendor should not silently replace the workroom's primary vendor.

Transactions — financial master data

Forklift Transactions is the financial master. If money was spent on a forklift and should be part of forklift reporting, the cost should ultimately exist here. Negative transactions/credits reduce totals.

Transactions drive yearly/lifetime expense, repair spend, PM spend, rental spend, service-call counts, Budget actuals, Aging / Grading inputs, and portions of Replacement Planning.

Common categories include Repair / Expense, PM Service, Rental, and other approved classifications. PM and rental costs remain real operating expense but are excluded from the Aging / Grading repair-cost score.

Double-click a transaction to correct the forklift, historical workroom, vendor, invoice/reference, amount, category, service-call flag, hours, downtime, rental requirement, description, URL, or notes. Correct the existing record rather than deleting/reimporting whenever practical.

What exactly counts as a Service Call?

A Service Call represents the repair visit/event, not every accounting line on an invoice. A multi-line invoice may contain labor, parts, freight, and fees, but normally only one line for that repair event should carry Service Call = Yes.

PMs always use Service Call = No. Rental-only transactions also do not count as service calls. This prevents service-call history from being inflated by accounting detail.

Major Life-Extending Repair (MLER)

Use MLER only when a repair materially restores a major component and is expected to extend useful life. It is a manual management classification; the app does not assign it from dollar amount alone.

Major Repair Notes are required. The full repair cost remains in actual yearly/lifetime spend, but only 50% of an MLER's repair cost enters the Aging / Grading repair calculation before year weighting.

Replacement Planning also evaluates recent MLER performance. A successful recent MLER with little post-repair spend/service activity can defer a suggested replacement; continued repair activity after the MLER reduces that benefit.

NetSuite CSV import

The monthly NetSuite importer is invoice/document-centered. Review the proposed action before anything is saved. The importer matches forklift information from the accounting memo/known identifiers, preserves historical workroom assignment where lifecycle history exists, and checks existing Transactions for duplicates.

6022 is handled as forklift Repair / Expense or PM Service. PM suggestions use known PM patterns, but classification remains reviewable. A repair document can receive one Service Call suggestion; PM always remains Service Call = No.

6023 Rental: confirmed forklift-rental wording can be imported as Rental; ambiguous rental/equipment charges require review; obvious non-forklift equipment such as scissor/boom/aerial lifts should be excluded. Rental-company equipment numbers are never treated as Forklift Manager IDs. Multi-line 6023 invoices are consolidated to one Rental transaction and can reconcile an existing open rental.

Use Exclude — Accounting Miscoding, Exclude — Not Forklift Related, or Exclude — Other when the accounting document should not enter Forklift Manager. Exclusions are preferable to forcing bad accounting data into the database.

Multiple NetSuite lines can reconcile to a transaction/document already entered through a service or rental workflow. Always review the import preview before clicking Import Approved Documents.

Avoiding duplicate expenses

If a PM invoice upload creates a PM transaction, a repair update records known invoice expense, or a rental is completed with known cost, the financial record may already exist before the monthly NetSuite import.

The importer checks invoice/date/forklift/amount relationships and supports many accounting lines reconciling to an existing transaction. Matching records should be skipped/reconciled rather than created again.

For normal repairs, it is usually best to open/manage the Repair Case without guessing cost and let NetSuite create the financial record when the invoice posts.

Repair Cases — the full workflow

Open the case when the problem is reported. No invoice or cost is required. Record the issue, request date, reporter when useful, vendor, current case status, forklift availability, and any confirmed initial service date.

Manage the same case through statuses such as New Request, Service Scheduled, Diagnosing, Waiting on Quote, Waiting on Approval, Waiting on Parts, Follow-Up Service Needed, Replacement Recommended, Repaired / Resolved, Parked, Retired / Disposed, or Cancelled / No Longer Needed.

Availability, Fleet Status, and Rental Need are separate. A forklift may be Usable, Limited Use, or Down / Unusable without automatically requiring a rental.

Add technician visits/updates to the existing case. Updates can store vendor, work-order/invoice reference, hours, downtime, attachments, notes, rental information, and an optional Fleet status change. A Fleet status change also creates lifecycle history.

Close the case only when there is a final outcome. Replacement Recommended stays open until management makes a final operating/disposition decision.

PM / Work Order / Invoice upload

This workflow accepts PM work orders, PM invoices, and other forklift service documents. The PDF reader looks for serial number, meter hours, work-order/invoice reference, completion/start date, vendor indicators, PM wording, and invoice amount where available.

Serial matching is preferred when a reliable serial is found. Date Completed is preferred over Date Started. You review/correct the proposed match before saving.

A PM upload creates/reuses a PM Service Event and stores the document as a SharePoint attachment. If hours are present and accepted, Fleet Current Hours and Hour Readings are updated. If a real invoice amount is confirmed, the workflow can create the PM Service transaction immediately; otherwise NetSuite can create the financial transaction later.

Do not invent missing hours or cost. Leave a field blank when the document does not support it.

PM Schedule — evidence, right-sizing, Next PM Due, and Frequency Review

PMs are managed on the separate PM Schedule page, not on Dashboard Needs Attention. The PM page includes only Active and Down for Repair forklifts assigned to an active Lease Manager workroom. Parked, Retired, Sold, Scrapped, and inactive-workroom units are excluded from the active PM table.

Last PM evidence: PM Service Events are preferred; reliable PM Transactions are the fallback. Service Event and Transaction records within about 3 days are treated as the same PM visit. Hours at Last PM come from the PM record itself or a nearby Hour Reading when available.

Observed Frequency describes historical cadence. The app uses the median spacing between PM visits: Monthly 20–45 days, Quarterly 70–105, Every 4 Months 106–150, Biannual 151–225, Annual 300–430. Two visits produce an inferred pattern; repeated consistent intervals can become History Confirmed. Manual Confirmed schedules remain user-controlled.

Recommended Frequency is hours-driven. Measured meter usage is the strongest input. The app requires at least two valid readings spanning at least 45 days and calculates operating hours per weekday. About 3.25+ hr/workday recommends Quarterly; roughly 1.75–3.24 supports Every 4 Months; below 1.75 can support Biannual only when recent repair/service history is clean.

When measured hour history is not mature, YTD installs are only a fallback utilization proxy. Charlotte is treated as Very High utilization; DFW and Norcross as High utilization. Other workrooms with 1,500+ installs YTD are treated as High. High/Very High proxy use recommends Quarterly; otherwise the conservative fleet baseline is Every 4 Months until measured hours prove otherwise.

Condition can tighten but never loosen the usage recommendation. High signals include 12,000+ hours, age 15+, $3,000+ Repair YTD, or 3+ Service Calls YTD. Moderate signals are shown for monitoring.

Next PM Due = Last PM + Recommended Frequency. Frequency Review shows Keep, Frequency Change Recommended, or Set Recommended. The recommendation does not silently overwrite an approved/manual schedule.

The PM table is sortable by clicking column headers and searchable/filterable. Double-click a forklift to open PM History showing visit dates, hours, source, vendor/reference, and spacing between PMs. Recalculate PM Data refreshes the derived schedule from current Fleet, Transactions, Service Events, and Hour Readings.

Hour Readings and Current Hours

Forklift Fleet stores the latest Current Hours; Forklift Hour Readings preserves the historical meter trail. Readings may come from Manual Update, PM, Repair, or Work Order workflows.

Duplicate guards prevent the same forklift/date/hours reading from being created twice. A lower meter reading requires confirmation.

Hours affect both Aging / Grading and PM right-sizing. PM utilization becomes much more reliable once each lift has at least two valid readings 45+ days apart, so continuing to capture hours from every useful work order/PM is important.

Rentals — status, cost, invoice, and NetSuite reconciliation

Rental need is separate from repair-case status and forklift availability. Use Rental Needed / Approved only after the decision is made to obtain a rental.

Rental Status tracks Requested, Active, Returned, or Cancelled. Cost Status separately tracks Pending or Complete. A rental may remain open with cost blank while you wait for the invoice.

Double-click a rental in Open Rentals / Missing Cost to enter final cost/reference/vendor or attach the invoice. When cost becomes Complete, the app creates a Rental transaction unless a matching one already exists.

NetSuite 6023 imports can match/reconcile the existing rental. Rental Spend YTD and Rental Days YTD feed management reporting and Replacement Planning; rental expense is excluded from Aging / Grading repair-cost points.

Forklift Budget — 6022 vs 6023, forecasts, close, and reopen

The Budget page keeps 6022 Repair & Maintenance and 6023 Forklift Rental as separate monthly budget pools. Rental budget is never treated as available repair budget.

Accounting Actual comes from Forklift Transactions. Forecast exposure is built from currently known open repair cases, expected PMs, and rentals. Open-repair estimates use historical repair experience, issue type, case status, and timing. Manual forecast overrides can be recorded with a reason.

The page shows the selected month plus a forward outlook and explains what is driving the projection. A future month is forecast-only. The current month combines actuals with known future exposure.

Close Month is used after the monthly NetSuite import has been completed/reviewed. Closing saves the month-end forecast/actual snapshot for later accuracy review. If accounting actuals change after close, the page flags that change. Reopen Month requires a reason.

Annual budgets can be uploaded from Excel. When the requested year's budget is not present, the app can fall back to the latest prior-year budget for the same month/account until a new budget is provided.

Leadership PDF and Excel Detail report 6022 and 6023 independently and include the underlying actual/forecast drivers.

Aging / Grading — scoring rules

Aging / Grading measures current condition/risk. It combines weighted recent Repair Cost, weighted Service Calls, and Total Hours. PM and rental expense are excluded from repair-cost grading; PM and rental-only records never count as service calls.

Repair weighting: current year 100%, prior year 50%, two years prior 25%. Weighted Repair points: $0 = 0; $1–$1,499 = 1; $1,500–$2,999 = 2; $3,000–$5,999 = 3; $6,000+ = 4.

Service-call weighting: current year 100%, prior year 75%, two years prior 50%, three years prior 10%. Weighted Service Call points: under 1.00 = 0; 1.00–2.99 = 1; 3.00–4.99 = 2; 5.00–6.99 = 3; 7.00+ = 4.

Hours points: under 2,500 = 0; 2,500–5,999 = 1; 6,000–8,999 = 2; 9,000–11,999 = 3; 12,000+ = 4.

Grades: 0–3 Excellent; 4–6 Good; 7–8 Watch; 9+ Replace Soon. Replace Soon is a management signal, not an automatic replacement order.

Lifetime Repair Spend is context, not a score component. When Estimated Replacement Cost exists, lifetime repair spend under 50% has no lifetime flag; 50–99% = Elevated Lifetime Spend; 100%+ = High Lifetime Spend.

The screen shows both actual current-year repair spend and the weighted repair amount used for scoring so financial reality is never confused with the grading formula.

Replacement Planning — suggested timing, rightsizing, and management plan

Replacement Planning is separate from Aging / Grading. The grade measures condition/risk; Replacement Planning translates that evidence into capital timing. A management planning decision never changes the underlying grade.

The system considers weighted repairs, weighted service calls, hours/age, reliable rental/downtime burden, lifetime-spend context, historical reliability context, MLER performance, and the number of forklifts actually required at each workroom.

Workroom targets currently include Charlotte 2, Norcross 2, Houston 1, DFW 2, and Fredericksburg 1; other workrooms default to one unless separately configured. If a workroom has more forklifts than its target, a rightsizing removal can carry $0 replacement capital.

The automatic forecast is intentionally conservative: a maximum of 4 suggested replacement purchases per year, using a $9,000 fleet-average planning cost. A unit-specific Estimated Replacement Cost remains visible for context but does not replace the planning assumption in the capital forecast.

Management can Use Suggested Year, Plan Specific Year, Monitor / Defer, or mark No Replacement Needed. Overrides require a planning note/reason and preserve Last Reviewed. Planned decisions are stored with the forklift and survive normal Fleet edits.

Parked units do not receive automatic replacement recommendations. Successful recent MLERs may defer timing; continuing repair/service activity after an MLER reduces the deferral benefit. Stale/missing hours lower planning confidence rather than making a lift appear healthier.

Replacement Planning includes fleet detail, leadership forecast, Excel export, and Leadership PDF.

Reports and exports

Aging / Grading: Excel provides detailed scoring/audit history; Leadership PDF provides a concise management view. ROM filtering is respected when selected.

Lifecycle History: can be exported for the filtered history or for an individual forklift, including effective date, before/after state, source, void status, user metadata, notes, and disposition amount.

Forklift Budget: Leadership PDF and Excel Detail report monthly 6022/6023 actuals, projections, remaining/risk status, and forecast drivers.

Replacement Planning: Excel exports detailed planning plus forecast summary; Leadership PDF shows replacement years, purchase counts, planning capital, actions, priorities, and reasons.

Lease Manager integration and active workrooms

Active workroom choices are pulled from Lease Manager to reduce spelling drift and keep the Forklift Manager aligned with the property database.

Active, Down for Repair, and Parked forklifts must normally be tied to an active Lease Manager location. Retired/Sold/Scrapped units may retain an inactive historical location.

The PM Schedule is stricter: it shows only Active/Down for Repair lifts whose current workroom is active. Historical service and financial records are not retroactively moved when a forklift transfers; lifecycle history determines the location that applied on the event date.

What updates automatically?

After a save/import, the app reloads live SharePoint data and recalculates dependent views. Examples include yearly/lifetime transaction totals, grading metrics, Budget actuals/forecast drivers, PM evidence and Next PM Due, and Replacement Planning analysis.

A new PM Service Event or a newly imported PM Transaction can move Last PM forward. New Hour Readings can change PM utilization/right-sizing, Aging / Grading hours points, and replacement-planning confidence. New repair/rental Transactions can change grading, budget actuals, and planning evidence.

Automatic recalculation does not mean every management choice is overwritten. Manual PM schedule confirmation, lifecycle state changes, MLER designation, Budget manual overrides, and Replacement Planning management decisions remain controlled actions.

Common warnings and what they mean

Lower Hours: the proposed reading is below the current/prior meter. Continue only when the old reading is wrong or the meter was replaced/reset.

Primary Vendor mismatch: the event vendor differs from the workroom's Primary Service Vendor. Decide whether it is a one-off vendor or a true workroom vendor change.

Duplicate / existing invoice: the document or accounting line may already exist. Normally reconcile/skip rather than create a second expense.

Rental Cost Pending: the rental exists operationally but final cost has not yet been completed/reconciled.

No PM History / Schedule Missing / Frequency Change Recommended: these are PM Schedule review states, not Dashboard failures. Use the PM page to see the evidence and recommendation.

Lifecycle integrity warning: current Fleet state conflicts with the latest non-void lifecycle history, a final disposed unit still has open operational items, or history exists after a final disposition. Review before changing records.

Tracking Prevention / favicon 404: usually browser/privacy or missing tab-icon messages and not a data-save problem when the app otherwise loads/saves normally.

Recommended monthly operating routine

During the month: open Repair Cases as problems are reported; upload PM/service documents as they arrive; record useful meter readings; manage rentals and lifecycle changes when they happen.

When NetSuite is available: import the monthly file, review every document classification/match/exclusion, reconcile duplicates and 6023 rentals, then verify the Transactions view.

After accounting review: review the PM Schedule for overdue/due-soon/frequency changes, review Forklift Budget and close the month when appropriate, then use Aging / Grading and Replacement Planning for management review.
